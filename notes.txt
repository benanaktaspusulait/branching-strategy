# PROMPT

You are a Principal Enterprise Architect, former AWS Principal Architect, and Architecture Review Board (ARB) chair.

Review the attached Cerberus Release Engineering Assessment and Enterprise Knowledge Graph proposal as if you are preparing it for presentation to:

* Chief Architect
* Enterprise Architecture Review Board
* CTO
* Head of Engineering
* Senior Engineering Managers
* Platform Architecture Team

Your task is NOT to rewrite the document from scratch.

Instead, perform a professional architecture review and produce an enhanced version that is suitable for executive and ARB approval.

Focus on:

## 1. Architecture Quality Review

Identify:

* gaps
* weaknesses
* assumptions
* architectural risks
* missing governance
* missing ownership models
* missing operational considerations
* missing security concerns
* missing scalability concerns
* missing data quality concerns

For every issue found:

* explain why it matters
* describe the business impact
* propose a concrete improvement

---

## 2. Executive Readability Review

Determine whether:

* a CTO can understand the proposal within 5 minutes
* an ARB member can make a decision within 15 minutes
* a Platform Lead can understand implementation implications

Improve:

* executive summaries
* architecture narratives
* key messages
* business justification

Produce:

### Executive Summary (1 page)

containing:

* Current State
* Problems
* Risks
* Recommendations
* Expected Outcomes
* Investment Required
* Decision Required

* contradictions
* overlapping responsibilities

Create:

### Architecture Alignment Report

showing:

* Issue

* Recommendation

Bu prompt özellikle ARB, Chief Architect ve Principal Architect seviyesinde "eksik ne kaldı?" analizini almak için oldukça güçlü. Şu anki dokümanının seviyesini objektif olarak ölçmek için de kullanılabilir.

You are performing a critical architecture review of all documents in the Cerberus Release Engineering Assessment and Knowledge Graph proposal.

Important Context:

This is NOT a normal enterprise application.

Cerberus is a mission-critical platform used within the UK border security ecosystem.

Assume:

* Very high operational importance.
* National-security-adjacent environment.
* Multiple dependent systems.
* Large-scale data processing.
* Approximately 5+ billion records per month.
* Multiple environments.
* Multiple engineering teams.
* Strict audit requirements.
* High availability requirements.
* Very low tolerance for deployment mistakes.
* Significant operational and reputational impact from outages.
* Large blast radius if release engineering decisions are wrong.

Your task is NOT to improve writing quality.

Your task is to challenge the architecture recommendations.

For every recommendation in the document perform a suitability assessment.

---

# Step 1

Create a table:

| Recommendation | Suitable | Needs Modification | Not Recommended | Reason |

Review every major recommendation including:

* Branch strategy simplification
* Release branch automation
* Drone migration
* Validation framework
* Deployment-management evolution
* Release reporting
* Release ownership model
* Unified deployment control plane
* Knowledge Graph
* Engineering Copilot
* Event-driven architecture
* Rollback recommendations
* Environment promotion model
* Progressive delivery references
* GitOps references
* Changed-chart deployment
* Approval workflow automation

Do not assume they are correct.

Challenge them.

---

# Step 2

Perform a "Scale and Criticality Assessment".

For each recommendation evaluate:

## Operational Safety

Would this increase or decrease operational risk?

## Blast Radius

If it fails, how many systems could be affected?

## Recovery Complexity

How difficult would recovery be?

## Human Factors

Would operators understand it during an incident?

## Auditability

Would it improve or weaken traceability?

## Security Impact

Would it increase attack surface?

## Platform Complexity

Would it make the platform harder to operate?

Score each category from:

1 (Poor)
to
5 (Excellent)

Provide rationale.

---

# Step 3

Challenge Automation Assumptions.

Look for any recommendation that assumes:

* More automation is always better.
* Fewer manual approvals are always better.
* Faster deployment is always better.
* GitOps is always better.
* Progressive delivery is always better.
* Centralisation is always better.

For each assumption:

Explain:

* Why it may be valid.
* Why it may be dangerous in a border-security environment.
* What safeguards would be required.

---

# Step 4

Challenge Rollback Assumptions.

Review all rollback-related recommendations.

Assess:

* Data consistency risks.
* Liquibase implications.
* Cross-system dependency risks.
* Partial rollback scenarios.
* Event replay implications.
* Kafka message implications.
* Downstream system impacts.

Identify where "rollback" is unrealistic and where "fix-forward" may be safer.

---

# Step 5

Challenge Knowledge Graph Recommendations.

Assume:

* Hundreds of services.
* Hundreds of engineers.
* Large event volumes.
* Long retention periods.

Evaluate:

* Data freshness risks.
* Incorrect relationship risks.
* Metadata quality risks.
* Governance complexity.
* Cost of maintenance.
* Security classification concerns.

Recommend whether:

A) Build immediately
B) Build later
C) Run limited pilot
D) Avoid entirely

Provide justification.

---

# Step 6

Challenge Engineering Copilot Recommendations.

Assume:

* Security-sensitive environment.
* Operational decision making.
* High audit requirements.

Assess:

* Hallucination risk.
* Incorrect recommendation risk.
* Trust model.
* Evidence requirements.
* Access control implications.

Identify which copilot capabilities should remain prohibited.

---

# Step 7

Identify Missing Enterprise Concerns.

Specifically look for missing discussion of:

* Disaster recovery
* Multi-region considerations
* Operational resilience
* Data sovereignty
* Security accreditation
* Capacity planning
* Incident command model
* Change advisory processes
* Separation of duties
* Privileged access controls
* Service ownership at scale

Add recommendations where appropriate.

---

# Step 8

Produce a "Border Security Reality Check".

Create a section:

## Recommendations That Are Potentially Too Aggressive

List recommendations that may be appropriate in commercial SaaS environments but require modification in a border-security platform.

Explain why.

---

# Step 9

Produce a revised recommendation table.

Create:

| Original Recommendation | Revised Recommendation | Reason |

Make recommendations more conservative where required.

---

# Step 10

Provide a final verdict.

Answer:

"If this platform genuinely processes 5+ billion records per month and supports UK border-security operations, which recommendations would you still strongly support, which would you modify, and which would you defer until much later?"

Be brutally realistic.

Prefer operational safety over engineering elegance.

Prefer auditability over convenience.

Prefer resilience over automation.

Prefer controlled evolution over large architectural change.
